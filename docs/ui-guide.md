# Driving the UI

Start the server and open `http://localhost:9090/ui`. Breakdown fetches every metric's series from the provider at startup, so the first load takes a few seconds. The steps below use the bundled default `breakdown/examples/jaffle_shop_tree.yml` and its `2024-01-01` to `2024-04-09` window; substitute your own target and dates. The header date pickers are bounded to the loaded `--start-date`/`--end-date` window.

**1. Inspect a metric and fit its model.** Click any node in the graph to open the **Metric** tab (right sidebar) with its time series. Nodes that have a probabilistic parent (e.g. `order_count`) show an **Analyze** section. Pick **NUTS — exact** (the default) or **ADVI — fast, approximate** and click **Run** to fit the BSTS. Both controls are labelled, a line under the draws box says what the current setting actually costs, and the `ⓘ` beside each expands a short explanation, with a link into [docs/model.md](model.md) where it goes deeper. The posterior fills in: trend, seasonality, and the `beta` / `beta_raw` coefficient on each parent. The node picks up the "fitted" tint. Leaf and formula nodes just show their series. An ADVI fit also reports **PSIS k̂** beside R̂ — how far the fast approximation landed from the posterior it approximates, in a few words rather than a bare number ("close to the posterior", "measurably off", "not usable"). Above 0.7 the approximation is not evidence about the width of its own intervals; re-run that metric with NUTS. The number carries its own error (`1.36 ± 0.22`) because k̂ is estimated from a sample too, and where the estimate lands within one of those errors of a band edge the line says **band unresolved at this error** — read the worse of the two bands, and settle it with NUTS if it matters. A NUTS fit has no k̂ and needs none, which is why most fits you see here show R̂ and nothing else. Under the posterior, a **Posterior predictive check** panel draws the series the model was fitted on against the 50% and 95% bands of the series that model generates — the check the engine states as a verdict, left in the shape you can actually look at. Where the band contains the line, the model can reproduce this history; where it does not, you can see which periods. Nothing in it is coloured by the verdict, so read the geometry: on the demo tree's `trials_started`, a count that never drops below 6, the band's lower edge runs below zero, which is the whole of what the warning above it says in words. The caption states which periods were fitted (a model fitted for an RCA stops before the analysis window, so its chart does too), and on a formula node it says the line is the identity's **residual** rather than the metric. Long daily series are denser than the panel has pixels — drag across a stretch to zoom in, double-click to reset. A node with no fit, or one whose check could not run, says so in words rather than showing an empty chart.

**2. Run a root-cause analysis.** Choose a **Target** in the header bar, then open the **Root cause** tab and pick the **Analysis** window, the period you want explained, from a preset (Last 7 days, Last 14 days, Last full week) or the date pair. The **Reference** fills itself with the matched adjacent block (marked **auto**) and stays editable. Touch it and it becomes custom; the auto chip restores it. The model doesn't train on the reference. It trains on *all* loaded history before the analysis window (each node's result says exactly what it was fitted on), so the reference is only the baseline the gap is measured against. The **Fits** select picks the sampler for anything this run has to fit: **NUTS** (the default, exact) or **ADVI** (the fast approximation, for a tree wide or fine-grained enough that exact sampling is impractical — the line under it says what that costs, and every node it fits reports its PSIS k̂: a suspect one as a warning chip, a clean one as the figure beside the node's `posterior · ADVI` label). Every fitted node's header names the sampler that produced its numbers, and the exported report's Methods footnote names it too — two colleagues' differing runs are legible as a sampler choice rather than drift. Click **Run RCA**. Breakdown auto-fits any upstream probabilistic models it needs, with exact MCMC — so expect a minute or more per learned node on a cold cache — and paints the result on the graph: nodes tinted by direction of change, edges weighted by each parent's share of the explained gap (thicker = larger share, fainter = less certain the direction; the red/green hue appears only where the child metric declares a [`direction`](yaml-reference.md), since good/bad is the tree's claim to make, not the renderer's), and a ranked cause list with credible intervals. Clicking a ranked cause glows the path from that cause up to the target — a highlight laid under the colors, so the attribution encoding stays readable while you inspect it. **Copy link** yields a shareable `#rca=…` URL carrying the resolved windows; **Clear** resets.

**3. Localize a cause with a slice (optional).** Any ranked cause whose metric declares [`dimensions`](yaml-reference.md#dimensions-slicing) shows a **slice by** row. Click a dimension to attribute that metric's gap across its values. Slices are ranked by excess concentration: how much more of the gap a value carries than its baseline share predicts. The leader is highlighted when it is the finding, values where the bootstrap can't separate concentration from zero get a `noise` badge, and when nothing stands out the verdict is an explicit "not localized". A third verdict covers the case where the concentration is real but sits in `everything else` — the roll-up of the values outside the dimension's `top_k`: the panel says the gap is concentrated in the long tail and tells you to raise `top_k` (or slice another dimension) to see inside it, rather than naming a bucket you can't act on. Across a lagged edge the slice uses the parent's lag-shifted windows, so it compares the periods the contribution was actually measured over.

**4. Simulate a what-if (optional).** Open the **What-if** tab and click nodes to adjust them (interventions). Add assumption links for effects the tree doesn't encode if you need them, then click **Run simulation** for a steady-state projection with credible intervals, rendered on the graph and in the sidebar.

RCA runs and metric views are deep-linkable (`#rca=…`, `#metric=…`), so you can share or bookmark any analysis as a URL.
